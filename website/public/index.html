A way into
the machine

USB gave us nothing.
The robot’s own screen gave us a terminal.

Phone tethering brought it online. A reverse SSH tunnel through our VPS brought its screen to our laptops. Then we installed Codex CLI on the D1 itself.

Codex helped us explore the vendor’s ROS graph, build the bridge and drive software, and work through the real hardware’s surprises. We mapped the system before sending a single motion command.

Open software.
Original hardware.

No firmware flashed. Nothing cut or replaced. We found the integration point the robot already had: the vendor’s ROS 1 graph.

13 sensor topics passed live delivery checks. The upper and lower depth cameras registered, but delivered no frames. Air-quality units and calibration remain unverified.

Built to stop

Movement came with boundaries. Every drive was supervised, with a person at the physical red stop.

250 ms

Without a fresh command,
the gate drops to zero.

Starts disarmed. Speed capped at 0.20 m/s and 0.25 rad/s.

We killed the drive software.

The independent watchdog tripped in 160 ms in the recorded test, requesting motor disable and power off.

Scan guards caught obstacles, including the robot’s own bracket. We measured those self-hits and filtered two narrow sectors.

The watchdog shares the host and network with the gate. The front laser cannot see behind the robot. The physical stop and human supervision remain essential.
